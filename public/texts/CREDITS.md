# Ink & Hour reading collection

The 12 complete English poems in `src/readings.ts` are original works by the
named authors, not translations, adaptations, or generated imitations. Their
poem bodies are bundled locally; neither playback nor the collection tests
fetch any third-party text. Source links are provenance, not runtime resources.
Sources consulted on 2026-09-11.

## Public-domain basis and source terms

The originals were first published between 1609 and 1824; their authors died
between 1616 and 1850. They are in the public domain in the United States and
the United Kingdom, and their authors' deaths also exceed life-plus-100-year
copyright terms. No copyright royalty or commercial-use permission is required
for these original poem texts in those jurisdictions. This is not a claim
about every country's other laws, later adaptations, or everything on the
source websites.

For the United States, even a full 95-year publication term has expired for
every original and every cited print edition (the latest edition is 1914).
See the U.S. Copyright Office's
[duration guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-duration.html) and
[17 USC 304](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap3.html#304).
The UK term for original literary works is generally the author's life plus
70 years: [UK government guidance](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/how-long-copyright-lasts).
Publication and death evidence for each selection is recorded below; `year`
means first publication of the poem, not composition, the later print edition,
or the date of an online transcription. Shelley's 1824 poem is explicitly
posthumous, not misdated as a lifetime publication.

Transcription credit: **Wikisource contributors**, using the identified
historical editions and page scans. Wikisource's
[reuse guidance](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Wikisource:Reusing_Wikisource_content)

Only the public-domain poem bodies are reproduced. No modern editorial

## Transcription and ASCII normalization

Poem wording, capitalization, punctuation, verse order, and stanza order follow
the specified source edition, not a mixture of familiar modern versions.
The following presentation-only normalization makes the text writable by the
app's printable-ASCII fonts:

- Curly quotation marks become straight ASCII quotes/apostrophes; an em dash
  becomes `--`. Existing hyphens and ampersands remain unchanged.
- Long-s typography in Blake's "bless" becomes ordinary `s`. Small caps,
  italics, and display capitals are rendered as the transcription's plain-text
  letters. There are no other ligature substitutions in this selection.
- Horizontal whitespace is normalized to single ASCII spaces; indentation,
  page-break markers, and line-edge whitespace are removed. Verse lines use LF
  newlines, and stanza breaks use one blank line. Printed page boundaries do
  not create extra stanzas.
- Titles, sonnet/stanza numbers, running heads, attribution signatures, and
  footnote markers are outside the poem bodies and omitted. Titles/authors are
  separate metadata. Familiar first-line titles identify otherwise untitled
  lyrics; they do not add a line to the poem.

No spelling corrections or word substitutions were made. In particular,
`my self`, `worth 's`, `wooly`, `sieze`, `waterd`, `veild`, `outstretchd`,
`shew`, `desart`, and `No thing` are intentional source readings. Unusual
capitalization and Ozymandias's original quotation-mark placement are retained.

### blake-the-lamb

**The Lamb** - William Blake; first published **1789** in *Songs of Innocence*;
author died **1827**.

Edition: *Songs of Innocence and of Experience*, **copy Z, 1826**, Library of
Congress; image/object 8, "The Lamb".
Source: <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Songs_of_Innocence_and_of_Experience,_copy_Z,_1826_(Library_of_Congress).pdf/8>

The [collection record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Songs_of_Innocence_and_of_Experience_(1826))

records Blake's death and lists *Songs of Innocence* (1789) with "The Lamb",
and *Songs of Experience* (1794) with "The Tyger" and "A Poison Tree".
`wooly` and ampersands are retained; the long-s in "bless" is normalized.

Edition: Wordsworth's *Poems*, Volume I, **1815**, "I wandered lonely".

Edition: *The Complete Poetical Works of Percy Bysshe Shelley*, edited by

Wikisource scan `The complete poetical works of Percy Bysshe Shelley, including materials never before printed in any edition of the poems.djvu`,
image 663, section s2 (printed p. 633).
Source: <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Complete_Poetical_Works_of_Percy_Bysshe_Shelley_(ed._Hutchinson,_1914)/To_%E2%80%94%E2%80%94._%27Music,_when_soft_voices_die%27>

or any of the later material mentioned in the collection's title.
The familiar first-line title is metadata; the em dash after "memory" becomes `--`.
